A. Design the dataset — before you touch a shelf

The dictated field list is gone on purpose: deciding what a price observation must capture is your job, and it's where the method actually gets built. Work it out as a team, then build the instrument once and use it identically.
What does a single price observation need to capture?

One row = one item at one store. List every field that row must hold for the number to be trustworthy and traceable later — think about what your analysis and your fact-check will demand of it, not just the price.

How will you capture it in the field?

A phone form that writes to the sheet, direct rows in the sheet, something else? Justify the choice — speed in the aisle vs. clean data vs. photos attached.

How do photos attach to the right row?

Every price should trace to a product photo and a price-tag photo. State your naming or upload scheme so a photo is never orphaned.

One "Regular" price per item per store — how do you enforce it?

Duplicates and missing cells are the two ways this data goes bad. How will you see gaps and avoid doubles as you go?
