A. Research question & index definition

Restate the question your index answers
Define "the index" precisely

A price table isn't an index until you define the comparison. A common shape: (1) the total basket cost per store, and (2) each store scored against the cheapest = 100, so 130 means 30% dearer. Add per-category sub-indexes if they earn their place. Decide it deliberately — this definition is load-bearing.

D. Your collection protocol — decide the rules, then both follow them

These are the rules that make the data comparable. Write them yourselves — don't wait to improvise in a store aisle. Both collectors follow the identical protocol, or the comparison breaks.
Date window

How few days can you collect the whole basket across, and how will you record the date and time of every price?

Which price you record

When a shelf shows a regular price, a sale, a loyalty price, and a unit price — which is your primary basket number, and how do you log the others so they don't distort it?

Substitution rule

Write it once, both follow it: when the exact spec isn't stocked, what exactly do you do? (e.g., cheapest comparable brand at the same size, clearly flagged — or mark N/A.)

Photo protocol & fallback

How many photos per item and of what? What's the plan if a store won't allow photos?

Anything else your method needs to stay honest

What did the prior pieces trip on that you'll handle up front? (Per-lb weighing, one price row per item per store, etc.)
