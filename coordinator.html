Coordinator's Guide

Running the Index

Principles to coach toward, a rigor bar for each phase, and context to steer with — guidelines, not a key to grade against.

What this is — and isn't. The playbook the interns read is deliberately thin: it hands them the mission and the phases, not the method. Deriving the method is the assignment. This page is your half — guidelines for coaching it, not an answer key. There is no single correct index here: two teams can pick different baskets, different stores, and different framings and both do excellent work. What you're checking is rigor, not conformity — is each choice deliberate, defensible, and traceable? Use this to ask better questions, not to grade against a fixed solution. Keep it to yourself, and offer a piece of it only when someone is genuinely stuck — a question beats an answer almost every time.

Your role

You are both roles the old draft split in two — coach and editor. You teach each phase, unblock the pair, review every deliverable, and give the final edit and sign-off before anything publishes. Everything routes to you. There is no second reviewer, so your approval is the gate.

A pace that lands it — offer it, don't impose it

The interns schedule their own week; that's part of the assignment. Hold this cadence only for your own situational awareness, so you can tell early whether the July 15 deadline is slipping. Weekdays only — no weekend work.

The rigor bar, phase by phase

These describe quality, not a specific right answer — two teams can clear the same bar with different methods. Approve when the "approve when" line is genuinely met; send it back with a specific note when you see the "send back if" pattern. Vagueness is the enemy, not divergence — a well-argued unconventional choice clears the bar; a lazy conventional one doesn't.

1Research BriefResearch & Context
Approve when
They can define a price index in plain words; the news peg is specific, not "prices are high"; all three prior BD pieces are read with a real gap named for each; there are 2–3 outside comparables with method noted; 3–4 cited stats trace to primary sources (an agency, a comptroller's report — not a secondhand number); the Mamdani facts are verified against primary sources; and the three angles are genuinely different, not one idea in three coats.
Send back if
The peg is generic, the stats are secondhand, or the "three angles" are interchangeable.

Approve when

Send back if
The hypothesis is mush ("prices will vary"), the sourcing is a list without reasoning, or no risks are named.
3Methodology & Field PlanThe most important gate
Approve when

Send back if

Data-standard principles

These seven are principles a rigorous method tends to honor — and there are many valid ways to satisfy each one. They are not a handout; most are things the pair should reach on their own in Phases 3 and 4. Hold them as a lens when you read the methodology, and surface one as a question ("what happens when the exact brand is out of stock?") only if they've missed it entirely. A team that meets the intent of a principle by a different route has done it right.

  1. Standardize every item. A price means nothing unless it's the same thing everywhere: a brand rule, a size, and a pricing basis per item. "Milk" isn't comparable; "half-gallon 2% store brand" is. (The 2017 team got caught on pint-vs-quart half-and-half.)
  2. Record the kind of price. Regular shelf, sale, loyalty, or unit price. Regular is the primary basket number; the rest are logged separately so a sale doesn't distort the index.
  3. Define the index. Basket total per store, plus each store indexed to the cheapest = 100. Optional per-category sub-indexes. A table of prices isn't an index until the comparison is defined.
  4. Collect in a tight window. The whole basket across as few days as possible, with a date and time on every price. A store priced a week late gets a note.
  5. Handle missing items by a pre-agreed rule. A clearly flagged substitute, or N/A — decided in advance so both collectors do it identically. Never a quiet swap of size or brand.
  6. Two photos per item. Product and price tag. If a store bars photos, they write down the price and shelf-tag details. Photos uploaded the same day.
  7. Everything traces back. Every published number → a Price Data row → a photo. Every quote → a named source. Nothing publishes until each is confirmed.
Alcohol / the beer row. The legacy basket carries a specific beer (Six Point Sweet Action). The interns are rising high-school seniors and may be under 21 — they photograph the shelf tag only, never purchase, or drop the item and note why. Make sure this is explicit before fieldwork.

Why the basket and stores are seeded

Phase 3 hands the pair a basket and a store universe drawn from the 2017 and 2021 pieces. That's intentional: continuity is what lets a reader — and you — compare 2026 against the prior editions. Their job is to verify and refine, not to reinvent. Two things to watch:

  • ClosuresSeveral seeded stores may have closed since 2021 (Brooklyn's Natural is the flagged one). Insist they confirm each is open before assigning a visit — a store priced that no longer exists is a correction waiting to happen.
  • Coverage of the rangeThe comparison only says something if the stores span cheap to premium: discount chain, mid supermarket, produce specialist, premium/natural, bodega, national chain, and — if in scope — a delivery app. If they drop stores, make sure the span survives.

Story-context leads

Facts to steer with — still make them verify and cite, but you can point here when they're circling.

The Mamdani city-owned grocery plan

Five municipal stores, one per borough, built around keeping a core basket of staples cheap; the city owns the space and covers overhead while a private operator runs the store and passes savings on core staples to shoppers. La Marqueta in East Harlem is an identified site; a Brooklyn location is still being examined. Local hook: Councilmember Jennifer Gutiérrez (whose district includes the Bushwick area) has pushed to make the program permanent. The obvious question the index answers: how do the neighborhood's stores compare with what a public store promises on those staples? See Bushwick Daily's coverage of the plan, then go to primary sources.

Amazon & Whole Foods

In 2026 Amazon moved to wind down Amazon Fresh and Go, convert some sites to Whole Foods, open many more Whole Foods, and lean into same-day fresh delivery — it's now a top-three U.S. grocer, mostly online. This ties to both the Whole-Foods-in-Bushwick angle and the delivery-app pricing decision.

The inflation peg

Reporting points to NYC-metro food prices rising faster than the national rate over the last decade. Push them to the primary figure (e.g., the State Comptroller's food-price analysis), not a secondhand number.

One angle to offer only if they're stuck

Hold in reserve

"We priced the basket the city wants to make cheaper, and here's what it actually costs across Bushwick right now." It ties the data to the municipal-grocery story. Don't lead with it — the angle is theirs to find. Offer it as one option among several only if they've genuinely run dry, and make them argue for it in the pitch like any other.

The publish gate

You run this before anything goes live. It also lives on the interns' Phase 9 worksheet, but the sign-off is yours.

  • Final approval given on the draft (by you).
  • Every number traces to a Price Data row and a photo.
  • Methodology box present: basket, stores, dates, limitations.
  • Every quote confirmed with the named source; spellings and titles correct.
  • Store names and addresses double-checked against the store list.
  • Charts (basket totals + index) and the store map embedded and labeled.
  • Photos captioned and credited.
  • Headline + dek finalized; SEO title/description set.
  • Data-availability / source note included.
  • Social posts and videos ready and scheduled.
  • Boost-account DMs drafted, ready to send after publish.
  • A plan to monitor the first 48 hours for comments and corrections.

Then: publish, post the package, send the boost DMs, and make sure the retro is captured in the Phase 9 worksheet.
