Exhibit 01 · 2021 → 2026

The $1.05 chicken that vanished

In November 2021, a pound of chicken breast cost $1.05 at Key Food and $15.99 at Food Story, six blocks apart. That 15.23× spread was the widest in the corpus, and the $1.05 sat 70.98% below the national average: consistent with a sale price caught on visit day. Toggle to 2026 and the anomaly is gone. Every store prices between $5.99 and $10.99, and the neighborhood median runs 67.2% above the national average.

Survey year
Read the fine print (7)
  1. The 15.23× spread is a quality-mix spread, not a like-for-like markup: the endpoints are a conventional store brand (Urban Meadow) versus a natural market's single premium option.
  2. Key Food's $1.05/lb is 70.98% below the BLS national average ($3.618/lb, Nov 2021) — consistent with a sale price on visit day. Retroactive proof is impossible from this corpus.
  3. 2021: Mr. Fruit Stores listed $13.99 per pack, unit unstated — excluded from the per-lb comparison. Hana Natural and Shop Fair carried none.
  4. 2026: chicken breast not carried at Hana, Brooklyn's Natural, or Food Story. Thin-sliced cutlets at 4 stores carry a per-lb premium over whole breasts by construction, so the 2026 level is overstated; the "no loss-leader" conclusion is safe, the +67.2% premium is soft.
  5. Mr. Lemon's $10.99 is D'Artagnan, regeneratively raised — flagged MISMATCH against the reference product.
  6. BLS figures are national averages, not NYC-metro item averages; single visit per store in every survey.
  7. Verdict per the 2026 analysis (B7): 7.61× Key Food's 2021 price against BLS breast +15.56% over the window — the durable-everyday-$1/lb reading of 2021 is dead; the visit-day sale remains the surviving explanation.

Exhibit 02 · 2021

The brand tax is bigger than the store tax

Switching stores changes your grocery bill by 2.5× to 3× on most staples. Choosing the premium brand on the same shelf changes it by up to 8.21×. The 2021 survey printed a low and a high for each item at each store; every bar below is one shelf. Tap a bar for the brand names behind it.

Mean within-store high÷low ratio per item (open-ended and unit-mismatch rows excluded from means). Coffee ratios are directional: pack sizes unstated. Tap an item to filter the shelves below.

Read the fine print (6)
  1. Coffee pack sizes are unstated in the source, so coffee ratios are directional in per-unit terms (chip: DIRECTIONAL).
  2. Open-ended rows ("$7.99 and up" at Hana, "$7.99 and up" at Met Fresh, "$8.39 and up" at Billy's) are valid floors only; the missing maxima are never drawn.
  3. City Fresh's milk row lists a gallon price under the half-gallon heading (chip: UNIT MISMATCH); its ratio is excluded from the item mean.
  4. Key Food's chicken low is the $1.05 sale-suspect from Exhibit 1.
  5. Associated is excluded: 5 of its rows are verbatim duplicates of Billy's in the source article (DUPLICATE DATA).
  6. Highs are absent for several stores; the range is what the article printed, not the full shelf.

Exhibit 03 · 2026

The 2026 shelf, item by item

The YJC index pinned one reference product per item and priced it at all 12 stores: 21 items, 245 priced observations, sales and comparability flags recorded. Pick an item. Solid mark = effective price (the sale price when lower). Whisker to a hollow tick = the shelf price before the sale. Tap a store row for the full receipt line.

Read the fine print (7)
  1. Single visit per store; effective prices embed visit-day sales (13 real discounts totaling $21.68 across the survey).
  2. One product recorded per store, so item medians overstate a cheapest-available premium; floors are the conservative bound.
  3. Comparability across the 252 rows: 156 OK · 39 Different · 42 Unclear · 15 Mismatch. Spread ratios whose endpoints are flagged are printed in italics.
  4. Eggs: Met Fresh's price is an 18-pack (MISMATCH as plotted; the analysis adjusts it to per-dozen separately). Billy's $1.67 is a 3-for-$5 sale.
  5. 7 of 252 rows are not carried (tofu at 3 stores, chicken breast at 3, beer single-bottles-only at Mr. Lemon) and render as dashed "not carried" slots, never as $0.
  6. 67 of 252 rows carry no collection date (all rows at Brooklyn's Natural, Food Story, Whole Foods, plus 4 others); their tooltips say so.
  7. BLS reference lines are national averages, not NYC-metro; they are drawn only where the item's unit matches the BLS series (eggs per dozen, boneless breast per lb). Milk is organic half-gallons and is never compared to the conventional-gallon BLS series.
