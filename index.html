What you're making

A grocery price index is a fixed basket of everyday items priced the same way at every store, so the comparison is honest: where the identical cart is cheapest, where it costs the most, and by how much, across Bushwick, Ridgewood, and the blocks around them. Bushwick Daily ran versions of this in 2017 and 2021. You are building the 2026 edition — better sourced, more current, and tied to a live policy fight.

What a finished piece owes the reader is narrow and demanding: the same items measured the same way at every store, every number traceable to a photograph and a spreadsheet row, real voices — an owner, an economist — explaining why the gaps exist, and a clear takeaway about where to shop and what it means. Consistency is the whole game: same basket, same rules, same week, every store.

Why it matters right now

Grocery affordability is the local story of the year, and a good index drops hard numbers into it. Food prices across the New York metro have outrun the national rate; Mayor Mamdani is standing up city-owned grocery stores built around a "core basket of staples"; and Amazon is redrawing the grocery map — winding down Fresh and Go, expanding Whole Foods, and pushing same-day delivery. Your basket is a ground-level version of the "core staples" the city says it can make cheaper.
